### **The Definitive Blueprint for the ViP-C Framework**

This document specifies the architecture, data flow, control loop, and training strategy for the ViP-C, a true hierarchical policy for multi-stage manipulation tasks.

#### **I. Core Philosophy: "State-Aware, Hierarchical Subgoaling"**

The ViP-C abandons the "static plan" of the Ego-Planner. Its philosophy is built on three unbreakable principles:

1.  **Dynamic Planning:** The plan is not a single thought computed at `t=0`. It is a *process* that unfolds over time. The high-level Planner re-evaluates the world state periodically to issue the next appropriate command.
2.  **Explicit State Conditioning:** The Planner's decisions *must* be explicitly conditioned on the critical physical state of the robot (e.g., `is_grasped`). This eliminates the ambiguity that led to the "cheating" behavior.
3.  **Grounded Subgoals:** The communication between the high-level Planner and the low-level Controller cannot be an abstract, entangled vector. It must be a concrete, unambiguous, and physically grounded instruction that the Controller can execute.

#### **II. High-Level Architecture & Data Flow**

The ViP-C is a single, end-to-end trainable `torch.nn.Module` composed of two distinct components:

1.  **The Planner (The "Foreman"):** A state-aware, high-level policy that looks at the big picture (`current_image`, `goal_image`, `is_grasped`) and decides **what to do next**. It runs periodically.
2.  **The Controller (The "Worker"):** A powerful, low-level diffusion policy that looks at the immediate surroundings (`observation_history`) and executes the Foreman's command. It runs at every control step.

The data flow is a constant dialogue:



1.  The **Planner** receives the current state and goal, and outputs a **Subgoal Affordance Heatmap**.
2.  A coordinate is extracted from this heatmap (`argmax`).
3.  The **Controller** receives this subgoal coordinate and the local observation history.
4.  The **Controller** outputs a short horizon of low-level actions, which are executed by the robot. This loop repeats until the subgoal is reached.

#### **III. Component Deep Dive: The Planner ("Foreman")**

*   **Objective:** To analyze the current world state in the context of the final goal and produce a visually grounded, spatially precise subgoal for the Controller to execute.

*   **Inputs (at decision time `t`):**
    1.  `current_image`: `[B, 3, 224, 224]` FloatTensor.
    2.  `goal_image`: `[B, 3, 224, 224]` FloatTensor.
    3.  `state_embedding`: `[B, 1, 768]` FloatTensor. A learnable embedding representing the current physical state. Example: `nn.Embedding(2, 768)` where index 0 is for `is_grasped=False` and index 1 is for `is_grasped=True`.

*   **Internal Architecture:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) based architecture.
    1.  **Shared ViT Backbone:** A frozen, pre-trained ViT (`google/siglip-base-patch16-224`) encodes `current_image` and `goal_image` into patch token sequences.
        *   **Shape:** `[B, 197, 768]`. We use the full sequence, not just the `[CLS]` token, to retain all spatial information.
    2.  **Fusion Transformer:** A standard, trainable `nn.TransformerEncoder` fuses the information.
        *   **Data Flow:** The input sequence is a concatenation of three distinct token types, each with its own learnable type embedding: `[current_image_tokens, goal_image_tokens, state_token]`.
        *   **Shape of input to Fusion Transformer:** `[B, 197 + 197 + 1, 768]`.
        *   **Output:** The Transformer processes this sequence. The final hidden states corresponding to the `current_image_tokens` are the most important, as they now contain context from the goal and the robot's physical state.
            *   **Shape:** `[B, 197, 768]`.
    3.  **Heatmap Generation Head:** This head transforms the contextualized visual tokens into a spatial affordance map.
        *   **Implementation:** A sequence of transposed convolutional layers (`nn.ConvTranspose2d`).
        *   **Data Flow:**
            a.  Take the patch tokens from the Fusion Transformer's output (ignoring the `[CLS]` token): `[B, 196, 768]`.
            b.  Reshape them back into a spatial grid: `[B, 768, 14, 14]`.
            c.  Pass this grid through the ConvTranspose layers to upsample it to a target size (e.g., `[B, 64, 56, 56]`).
            d.  A final `nn.Conv2d(64, 1, kernel_size=1)` collapses the channel dimension to produce the final heatmap.
*   **Output:**
    *   `predicted_heatmap`: `[B, 1, 56, 56]` FloatTensor. This is a probability distribution over the visual space, indicating the most salient point of interaction for the next subgoal.

#### **IV. Component Deep Dive: The Controller ("Worker")**

*   **Objective:** To receive a precise `(x, y)` subgoal coordinate from the Planner and generate a short, dynamically feasible, and grounded sequence of low-level motor commands to reach it. This is a **goal-conditioned behavior cloning policy.**

*   **Inputs (at every control step `t`):**
    1.  `observation_history`: A dictionary of FloatTensors, identical to your previous design (`image_primary`, `image_wrist`, `proprio`).
    2.  `subgoal_coordinate`: A `[B, 2]` FloatTensor representing the normalized `(x, y)` coordinate of the current subgoal.
    3.  `noisy_action_sequence`: `[B, H_a, D_action]` FloatTensor (for training).
    4.  `diffusion_timestep`: `[B]` LongTensor.

*   **Internal Architecture:** This is our powerful and proven **Grounded Action Decoder** from the `EgoPlanner`. We reuse it almost entirely.
    1.  **Real-Time Observation Encoders:** The same trainable `ResNetEncoder` modules for vision and an MLP for proprioception. They produce `tactical_tokens`.
    2.  **Subgoal Encoder:** A small, trainable MLP that projects the `[B, 2]` coordinate into a high-dimensional `subgoal_token` embedding.
        *   **Shape:** `[B, 1, 512]`.
    3.  **Denoising Core (Diffusion Transformer):** The exact same `EgoPlannerBlock` with AdaLN-Zero.
        *   **Data Flow & Unified Conditioning:** The `unified_context` for the cross-attention layers is a concatenation of the `tactical_tokens` from the real-time encoders and the new `subgoal_token`. This tells the controller where to go and what is around it right now.
*   **Output:**
    *   `predicted_noise`: `[B, H_a, D_action]` FloatTensor.

#### **V. The Hierarchical Control Loop (The "Dialogue")**

This is the runtime logic that brings the system to life.

1.  **Initialization (`t=0`):**
    *   The robot starts. The **Planner** runs once.
    *   `is_grasped=False`, so it predicts a heatmap with a peak on the red block.
    *   The `(x, y)` coordinate of this peak is extracted and stored as `current_subgoal`.

2.  **Controller Execution Loop (e.g., `t=0` to `t=30`):**
    *   At **every step**, the **Controller** runs.
    *   It takes the `observation_history` and the *same, static* `current_subgoal`.
    *   It outputs an action sequence, and the first action is executed.
    *   This continues, driving the robot towards the red block.

3.  **Re-Planning Trigger (e.g., at `t=31`):**
    *   The system detects a "re-plan" condition. This can be:
        a. The end-effector has reached the `current_subgoal` (within a tolerance).
        b. A fixed number of steps (`K`) have passed.
        c. A significant state change has occurred (the gripper has just closed: `is_grasped` flips to `True`).
    *   The **Planner** runs again.

4.  **Second Planning Step (`t=31`):**
    *   The **Planner** now sees the robot is near the block, and importantly, `is_grasped` is now `True`.
    *   It predicts a **new** heatmap, this time with a peak on the green goal mat.
    *   The coordinate of this new peak becomes the **new** `current_subgoal`.

5.  **Controller Execution Loop (e.g., `t=31` to `t=60`):**
    *   The **Controller** now runs at every step, but it is conditioned on the **new** subgoal coordinate, driving the robot (now holding the block) towards the goal mat.

6.  This cycle of **Plan -> Execute -> Re-Plan** continues until the task is complete.

#### **VI. Training Strategy**

*   **Data Engineering (The Prerequisite):** We must create a new `ViPCDataset`. For each sample (`current_image`, `state`, etc.), we must also provide the `ground_truth_subgoal_heatmap`.
    *   **Labeling Process:**
        1.  Analyze the expert trajectories to identify keyframes (e.g., start of grasp, end of placement).
        2.  For any timestep `t`, identify the *next* keyframe in the expert trajectory.
        3.  Get the 3D world coordinate of the end-effector at that future keyframe.
        4.  Project this 3D coordinate into the 2D pixel space of the `current_image` at timestep `t`.
        5.  Create a target heatmap by rendering a 2D Gaussian distribution centered at this projected pixel coordinate.

*   **Combined Loss:** The training objective is to minimize a weighted sum of the Planner's loss and the Controller's loss.
    ```python
    loss = (lambda_planner * loss_planner) + (lambda_controller * loss_controller)
    ```
    *   `loss_planner`: A pixel-wise MSE or BCE loss between the `predicted_heatmap` and the `ground_truth_subgoal_heatmap`.
    *   `loss_controller`: The standard diffusion MSE loss between `predicted_noise` and the target noise.

*   **Training Step (`forward` pass):**
    1.  The **Planner** takes `current_image`, `goal_image`, and `state_token` to produce `predicted_heatmap`. Compute `loss_planner`.
    2.  The **Controller** takes `observation_history` and the **ground-truth subgoal coordinate** (extracted from the ground-truth heatmap) as input. This is a form of "teacher forcing" that stabilizes training.
    3.  The **Controller** produces `predicted_noise`. Compute `loss_controller`.
    4.  Combine the losses and perform backpropagation.
