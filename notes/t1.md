### **The In-Depth Plan for the Ego-Planner Architecture**

This document serves as the technical specification for the unified, state-of-the-art model that will serve as the brain of your robot.

#### **I. Core Philosophy & Guiding Principles**

Before the architecture, we define the principles that dictate its design.

1.  **Disentanglement of Strategy and Tactics:** The model must separate the high-level, long-horizon "what to do" (Strategy) from the low-level, immediate "how to do it right now" (Tactics). This allows for both global coherence and real-time reactivity.
2.  **Maximal Information Bandwidth:** All planning decisions must be conditioned on the richest possible information. This means using full spatial feature grids from vision encoders, not just compressed single-vector embeddings.
3.  **Unified End-to-End Training:** The Strategist and Pilot are not trained separately. The entire model is a single neural network, trained with a single objective function. The loss signal from the tactical action predictions will backpropagate through the entire architecture, allowing the Strategist to learn what makes a "good" plan vector that leads to successful actions.
4.  **Hardware-Conscious Design:** While complex, the architecture is designed for efficiency. The most computationally expensive component (The Strategist) runs only once per task, while the real-time component (The Pilot) is designed to be lightweight enough to run at the robot's control frequency.

#### **II. The Unified Architecture: A Detailed Breakdown**

The Ego-Planner is a single `torch.nn.Module`.

##### **Component 1: The Contextual Plan Encoder (The "Strategist")**

*   **Objective:** To produce a single, dense `plan_vector` that encodes a globally-aware, bidirectionally-informed strategy for the entire task. This component is run **only once** at the beginning of each task.

*   **Inputs:**
    *   `initial_image`: `[B, 3, 224, 224]` FloatTensor. The first-person view at `t=0`.
    *   `goal_image`: `[B, 3, 224, 224]` FloatTensor. The desired final scene.

*   **Internal Architecture:**
    1.  **Shared Vision Backbone (ViT):**
        *   **Implementation:** A pre-trained Vision Transformer, such as `google/siglip-base-patch16-224` or `facebook/dinov2-base`.
        *   **Justification:** ViTs excel at capturing both semantic content and spatial relationships between different parts of an image. Using a large, pre-trained model provides a powerful, general-purpose vision frontend without the cost of training from scratch.
        *   **Parameter Sharing:** This backbone is applied to both the `initial_image` and the `goal_image`. This is parameter-efficient and ensures a consistent feature space.
        *   **State:** The backbone layers should be **frozen** during training to prevent catastrophic forgetting of their learned features.
        *   **Output:** We do **not** use the final pooled `[CLS]` output. Instead, we extract the sequence of patch embeddings from the final hidden layer.
            *   **Shape:** `[B, 197, 768]` (for a base-sized ViT, includes the `[CLS]` token). We will use all of them to retain maximum spatial information.

    2.  **Token Fusion Transformer:**
        *   **Implementation:** A standard `torch.nn.TransformerEncoder` consisting of multiple `nn.TransformerEncoderLayer` modules.
        *   **Justification:** This is the canonical architecture for fusing two sequences of information where every element needs to attend to every other element. It allows the model to reason about relationships like "the block in the *start image* needs to move to the empty space near the plate in the *goal image*."
        *   **Data Flow:**
            a. **Type Embeddings:** We create two learnable embedding vectors: `start_token_type` and `goal_token_type`. These are added to the patch embeddings from the ViT to explicitly inform the Transformer which sequence is which. This is a standard technique from models like BERT.
            b. **Concatenation:** The two sequences of patch embeddings (now with type embeddings added) are concatenated along the sequence dimension.
                *   `start_tokens` shape: `[B, 197, 768]`
                *   `goal_tokens` shape: `[B, 197, 768]`
                *   `fused_input` shape: `[B, 394, 768]`
            c. **Processing:** The `fused_input` is passed through the Transformer Encoder layers. Due to the self-attention mechanism, every patch from the start image can attend to every patch from the goal image, and vice-versa.
    *   **Output:**
        *   `plan_vector`: `[B, 768]` FloatTensor. This is the final hidden state corresponding to the `[CLS]` token from the *start image*'s sequence after it has passed through the fusion transformer. This token has aggregated information from the entire fused context.

##### **Component 2: The Grounded Action Decoder (The "Pilot")**

*   **Objective:** To take the strategic `plan_vector` and, conditioned on the *most recent* sensor data, generate a short, precise, and physically grounded sequence of actions. This component runs at **every time step** in a receding-horizon loop.

*   **Inputs (at each time step `t`):**
    *   `plan_vector`: `[B, 768]` FloatTensor. The static output from the Strategist.
    *   `observation_history`: A dictionary of FloatTensors, structured exactly like the output of your `HierarchicalControllerDataset`.
        *   `image_primary`: `[B, H_o, 3, 224, 224]` (Recent history of primary camera views)
        *   `image_wrist`: `[B, H_o, 3, 128, 128]` (Recent history of wrist camera views)
        *   `proprio`: `[B, H_o, D_proprio]` (Recent history of joint states, etc.)
    *   `noisy_action_sequence`: `[B, H_a, D_action]` FloatTensor (During training).
    *   `diffusion_timestep`: `[B]` LongTensor.

*   **Internal Architecture:**
    1.  **Real-Time Observation Encoders:**
        *   **Implementation:** These are directly adopted from your state-of-the-art `models/diffusion_policy.py`.
        *   **Vision Encoders:** Two separate, trainable `ResNetEncoder` modules for `image_primary` and `image_wrist`.
        *   **Justification:** ResNets are lightweight and excellent at extracting spatial features. Keeping them separate and trainable allows them to specialize to the specific visual information needed for low-level control (e.g., finger positions, object edges).
        *   **Proprioception Encoder:** A simple trainable `nn.Linear` layer or small MLP.
        *   **Fusion:** A Cross-Attention module (as in your `VisionFusionEncoder`) fuses the wrist and primary view features into a single sequence of `observation_tokens`.
            *   **Shape:** `[B, L_obs, 768]` (where `L_obs` depends on the flattened feature map size and `H_o`).

    2.  **Denoising Core (Diffusion Transformer):**
        *   **Implementation:** This is the `DiffusionTransformer` from your `models/diffusion_policy.py`, a `torch.nn.TransformerDecoder`-based architecture.
        *   **Justification:** It is already a state-of-the-art implementation for conditional sequence generation, which is exactly this task.
        *   **Data Flow & Unified Conditioning:**
            a. **Concatenate Conditioning Context:** We assemble the complete conditioning context by concatenating all available information sources into a single sequence.
                *   `plan_vector` (unsqueezed): `[B, 1, 768]`
                *   `observation_tokens`: `[B, L_obs, 768]`
                *   `proprio_tokens`: `[B, H_o, 768]`
                *   `unified_context` shape: `[B, 1 + L_obs + H_o, 768]`
            b. **Cross-Attention:** The `DiffusionTransformer`'s cross-attention layers take the noisy action sequence as the `query` and this `unified_context` as the `key` and `value`. This forces the model to ground its action predictions in the overall strategy, the current scene geometry, and its own physical state, all at once.
            c. **Timestep Conditioning:** The diffusion `timestep` is injected into each Transformer block using the AdaLN-Zero mechanism, exactly as you have implemented.

*   **Output:**
    *   `predicted_noise`: `[B, H_a, D_action]` FloatTensor.

#### **III. Training Plan & Data Requirements**

The Ego-Planner is trained end-to-end with a single diffusion loss.

*   **Dataset Requirements:**
    *   We will need to create a new `EgoPlannerDataset` class.
    *   Each item `__getitem__(idx)` from this dataset must return a tuple containing:
        1.  `initial_image`: The first frame of the episode.
        2.  `goal_image`: The final frame (or designated goal frame) of the episode.
        3.  `observation_history_chunk`: A standard chunk of `H_o` observations from a random valid timestep `t` in the episode.
        4.  `ground_truth_action_chunk`: The corresponding sequence of `H_a` actions starting from timestep `t`.
    *   Your existing `utils/expert_dataset.py` can be easily wrapped to provide this data. The `get_goal_image` method already exists, and the chunking logic is the core of its `__getitem__`.

*   **Training Forward Pass (`training_step`):**
    1.  Receive a batch of `(initial_image, goal_image, obs_history, gt_actions)` from the dataloader.
    2.  Pass `initial_image` and `goal_image` through the **Strategist (Plan Encoder)** to get the `plan_vector`. `plan_vector.detach()` could be used in early training stages to stabilize learning, but end-to-end is the goal.
    3.  Sample random `noise` and `timesteps`.
    4.  Create `noisy_actions` from `gt_actions`.
    5.  Pass `noisy_actions`, `timesteps`, `plan_vector`, and `obs_history` into the **Pilot (Action Decoder)** to get `predicted_noise`.
    6.  Calculate `loss = F.mse_loss(predicted_noise, noise)`.
    7.  Backpropagate the loss. Gradients will flow from the Pilot's output all the way back through the Strategist's layers.

*   **Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) during Training:**
    *   To enable CFG at inference, we must train the model to handle dropped conditioning. We will use two independent drop probabilities:
        *   `p_plan_drop`: With this probability, the `plan_vector` is replaced by a single, learnable `nn.Parameter` called `unconditional_plan_embedding`.
        *   `p_obs_drop`: With this probability, the `observation_history` tokens are replaced by another learnable `unconditional_obs_embedding`.
    *   This allows us to independently guide on the long-term plan and the short-term observations at inference time, providing fine-grained control over the robot's behavior.
